Update — Digital Omnibus on AI. The AI Act was amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force since 27 July 2026. Risk-based classification remains. The main changes are fixed and postponed dates for high-risk systems, two new prohibited practices, relief for SMEs and small mid-caps, and wider powers for the AI Office. The dates on this page already reflect these changes.

EU AI Act — European Regulation

Risk Classification

The European Artificial Intelligence Act classifies AI systems into four risk levels. Each level carries distinct obligations for organisations that develop or deploy AI within the European Union.

Minimal Risk

Free Use

The vast majority of AI systems in use today fall into this category. There are no conformity obligations specific to this level — organisations may develop and deploy them under existing legislation.

Even without specific obligations, the AI literacy duty applies (Art. 4). After the Omnibus, the duty consists of adopting measures but not yet, in this transitional phase, an obligation to demonstrate evidence of results. It remains mandatory.

Examples Spam filters, content recommendation engines, AI-powered video games, writing assistants, creative tools, basic support chatbots
What the law requires No conformity obligations specific to this category. Voluntary codes of conduct are encouraged. The AI literacy duty still applies to every system.
Organisational impact Low. Teams can keep innovating, while putting literacy measures in place for staff and for anyone operating AI on the organisation’s behalf.
✓ No specific conformity obligations — the literacy duty still applies
Limited Risk

Transparency
Obligations

Limited-risk systems must comply with specific transparency requirements — users have the right to know they are interacting with AI or consuming AI-generated content.

In force since 2 August 2026 (the Omnibus did not postpone these obligations). Generative systems placed on the market before that date have until 2 December 2026 to label synthetic content.

Examples Chatbots, deepfakes, and synthetic audio, video or image content
Disclosure obligation Users must be told when they are interacting with an AI system. AI-generated content must be labelled as such, except in contexts that are clearly artistic or satirical. Article 50 adds a duty to inform; emotion recognition itself is high-risk under Annex III and is prohibited in the workplace and in education.
Organisational impact Moderate. It means changes to interfaces and communications, without a heavy conformity process.
⚠ Duty to disclose AI interaction and to label AI-generated content
High Risk

Mandatory
Compliance

High-risk AI systems are subject to stringent requirements before they can be placed on the European market. They represent the core of the regulation and the greatest operational challenge for organisations.

Revised timetable (Omnibus): Annex III systems, including credit, insurance, employment and education, from 2 December 2027. Safety components of regulated products (Annex I) from 2 August 2028. The postponement allows more time; it does not change the requirements. Anyone who declares that a system is not high-risk (Article 6(3)) must still document that assessment and register the system, under a simplified procedure.

Covered domains Biometrics (Annex III, point 1), critical infrastructure, education and vocational training, employment and workers’ management, access to essential services — including credit and insurance —, law enforcement, migration and border control, administration of justice, and safety components of regulated products (Annex I)
Mandatory requirements Risk management, data governance, technical documentation, record-keeping, transparency towards users, human oversight, a conformity assessment by the provider, and registration in the EU database. Deployers of creditworthiness or credit-scoring systems, and of life and health insurance risk-assessment or pricing systems, must carry out a fundamental rights impact assessment (FRIA, Article 27).
What the Omnibus changed at this level Systems already on the market are caught only if they are substantially modified after the new date. The dates are fixed: the standards-readiness mechanism has been removed. A safety component is now a system whose purpose is to prevent or mitigate risks to health and safety. The Machinery Regulation moved from Section A to Section B of Annex I.
Organisational impact High. It calls for dedicated processes, documentation and governance — comparable to quality management in regulated sectors such as healthcare and finance.
⚠ Mandatory conformity assessment before the system is placed on the market
Prohibited Practices

Prohibited
in the European Union

These systems are deemed an unacceptable threat to fundamental rights and European Union values. Their use is strictly forbidden, with fines of up to €35 million or 7% of global annual turnover.

Alongside the prohibitions in force since February 2025, the Omnibus adds, from 2 December 2026, the generation of non-consensual intimate content of identifiable people and of child sexual abuse material.

Prohibited practices Social scoring by public or private entities; real-time remote biometric identification in publicly accessible spaces for law-enforcement purposes (with narrow exceptions); subliminal manipulation; exploitation of the vulnerabilities of specific groups
Also prohibited Emotion recognition in the workplace and in education; biometric categorisation based on sensitive characteristics (race, religion, sexual orientation, political opinions); untargeted scraping of facial images from the internet or CCTV to build facial-recognition databases; predictive policing based solely on profiling or on personality traits
Consequences of non-compliance Fines of up to €35 million or 7% of total worldwide annual turnover. For SMEs and small mid-caps, the lower of the two applies. Small mid-caps are a new category: companies that are not SMEs, with fewer than 750 employees and turnover of up to €150 million or a balance sheet of up to €129 million. The original prohibitions have applied since February 2025.
✕ Absolute prohibition — compliance is not possible

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (Artificial Intelligence Act), in force since August 2024, as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (Digital Omnibus on AI), in force since 27 July 2026.
